# Off-policy evaluation and offline policy learning for a small, session-grouped contextual-bandit log

Research date: 15 November 2026. Source status is marked per item: **[full text]** = I extracted and read the paper text; **[abstract]** = I opened only the arXiv/proceedings record and abstract; **[docs]** = documentation page; **[not opened]** = reported second-hand, named with the source that cites it. Where the arXiv record does not state a venue, the venue given is from my recollection and is marked "(venue not on record)".

## 1. Estimators for contextual bandits beyond IPS, SNIPS and DR: which reduce variance most at small sample sizes, and at what bias?

### Takeaway
No estimator beats IPS/DR in the worst case without trusting a reward model: IPS and DR are minimax-optimal up to constants when contexts rarely repeat, so every further variance cut (Switch-DR, DRos, clipping, MRDR) is bought with reward-model bias. On real logs the best-tuned shrinkage estimator (DRos) won at n = 300,000 but the plain direct method won at n = 10,000, so at a few thousand decisions the reward model's quality, not the choice among IPS variants, decides the outcome. MIPS/OffCEM target thousands of actions and offer little at 5 to 8 actions.

### Cited Findings

**Limits of what any estimator can do**
- In the agnostic contextual-bandit setting the minimax MSE is of order (E_mu[rho^2 sigma^2] + E_mu[rho^2 Rmax^2]) / n (Corollary 1 gives the lower bound with constant 1/700 for continuous context distributions), and both IPS and DR match it up to constants: "IPS and DR are unimprovable, in the worst case, beyond constant factors". DR is better than IPS "whenever r-hat is even moderately good". [full text] — [Wang, Agarwal, Dudik, 2017, ICML, "Optimal and Adaptive Off-policy Evaluation in Contextual Bandits"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01205)
- With a consistent reward model the best asymptotic risk is (1/n)(E_mu[rho^2 sigma^2] + Var_x E_pi[r|x]), with no dependence on Rmax; this is strictly below the agnostic lower bound. [full text] — [Wang, Agarwal, Dudik, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01205)
- For multi-armed bandits (one context) the regression estimator is minimax optimal up to a constant depending on the number of actions, while the other two standard estimators "can be arbitrarily worse". [abstract; the full paper was not read] — [Li, Munos, Szepesvari, 2015, AISTATS (PMLR 38:608-616), "Toward Minimax Off-policy Value Estimation"](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v38/li15b.html). Wang et al. restate this as minimax risk Theta(E_mu[rho^2 sigma^2]/n), achieved by DM, with IPS suboptimal, when each context recurs often enough to estimate its rewards by averaging — [Wang, Agarwal, Dudik, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01205)
- DR is accurate if either the reward model or the propensity model is good, and "uniformly improves over existing techniques" in their experiments. [abstract] — [Dudik, Langford, Li, 2011, ICML, "Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4601); [Dudik, Erhan, Langford, Li, 2014, Statistical Science 29(4):485-511, "Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Optimization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02834)
- DR can be worse than both IS and self-normalised IS when the Q-function is misspecified, and lacks SNIS's boundedness; empirical-likelihood estimators are proposed that are never less efficient than IS, SNIS and DR. [abstract] — [Kallus, Uehara, 2019, arXiv (NeurIPS 2019, venue not on record), "Intrinsically Efficient, Stable, and Bounded Off-Policy Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03735)

**Switch-DR**
- SWITCH uses IPS/DR where the importance weight is at most tau and the reward model where it exceeds tau; tau = 0 gives DM and tau to infinity gives IPS/DR. MSE bound: (2/n){E_mu[(sigma^2 + Rmax^2) rho^2 1(rho <= tau)] + E_pi[Rmax^2 1(rho > tau)]} + (E_pi[eps 1(rho > tau)])^2, where eps is the reward-model bias. It is "by design more robust to large (or heavy-tailed) importance weights"; tau is chosen from data by estimating variance and bounding bias. Evaluated on 10 UCI datasets converted to bandit feedback. [full text] — [Wang, Agarwal, Dudik, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01205)

**DR with shrinkage (DRos, and clipping as "pessimistic shrinkage")**
- Optimistic shrinkage replaces w by lambda*w/(w^2 + lambda) (lambda to 0 gives DM, lambda to infinity gives DR); pessimistic shrinkage is min(lambda, w), which "recovers (and justifies) existing weight-clipping approaches". lambda is chosen by minimising (bias upper bound)^2 + sample variance over a grid. Across 108 conditions (9 UCI datasets x 6 logging policies x 2 reward types) the shrinkage estimators had the best aggregate performance; with shrinkage the best reward predictor was a regression weighted by w^2, and MRDR was dominated by the other predictors apart from the zero predictor. [full text via ar5iv, first 100,000 characters only; the arXiv PDF exceeded the fetch size limit] — [Su, Dimakopoulou, Krishnamurthy, Dudik, 2020, ICML, "Doubly robust off-policy evaluation with shrinkage"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.09623)
- On the Open Bandit Dataset, DRos with automatic tuning was best for the three campaigns, "about 30-60% more accurate" than the second best, but the choice of lambda "greatly affects" it, and in the small-sample setting (n = 10,000) DM outperformed the other estimators while DRos was best at n = 300,000. The authors conclude a reliable data-driven way to choose and tune estimators is needed. [full text] — [Saito, Aihara, Matsutani, Narita, 2021, NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks, "Open Bandit Dataset and Pipeline"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07146)

**MRDR**
- MRDR fits the reward model by minimising the variance of the resulting DR estimator; proved strongly consistent and asymptotically optimal. [abstract] — [Farajtabar, Chow, Ghavamzadeh, 2018, arXiv (ICML 2018, venue not on record), "More Robust Doubly Robust Off-policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03493). Independent evaluations were less favourable: dominated by other predictors in [Su et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.09623), and among the direct methods weakened by policy mismatch in [Voloshin, Le, Jiang, Yue, 2019/2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06854).

**Self-normalised, clipped, balanced and sub-Gaussian weights**
- The self-normalised estimator fixes "propensity overfitting" of the unbiased counterfactual risk estimator. [abstract] — [Swaminathan, Joachims, 2015, NeurIPS 28, "The Self-Normalized Estimator for Counterfactual Learning"](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2015/hash/39027dfad5138c9ca0c474d71db915c3-Abstract.html). Self-normalised estimators "are no longer unbiased, but have variance bounded by r_max^2" and remain consistent. [docs] — [SCOPE-RL documentation](https://scope-rl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/evaluation_implementation.html)
- Clipping: Bottou et al. obtained "very consistent results in practice" clipping at the fifth largest observed weight, and report an inner confidence interval (uncertainty from the poorly explored region removed by clipping) and an outer one (uncertainty from sample size). [full text] — [Bottou, Peters, Quinonero-Candela, Charles, Chickering, Portugaly, Ray, Simard, Snelson, 2013, arXiv v5 (JMLR 14, venue not on record), "Counterfactual Reasoning and Learning Systems"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.2355). Replacing p by max{p_min, p} "may introduce a small bias" but can drastically cut variance. [full text] — [Li, Chen, Kleban, Gupta, 2014, arXiv (WWW 2015 companion, venue not on record), "Counterfactual Estimation and Optimization of Click Metrics for Search Engines"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1891)
- Capped and normalised importance sampling gave unsatisfying bias-variance compromises in personalised recommendation, where the action space is combinatorial. [full text, partial] — [Gilotte, Calauzenes, Nedelec, Abraham, Dolle, 2018, WSDM, "Offline A/B testing for Recommender Systems"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.07030)
- Balanced weights chosen to minimise worst-case or posterior conditional MSE avoid rejecting data that disagree with the target policy; the setting assumes the historical policy is unknown. [abstract] — [Kallus, 2018, arXiv (NeurIPS 2018, venue not on record), "Balanced Policy Evaluation and Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07384)
- Vanilla IS has an anticoncentration bound when policies are dissimilar; a power-mean transformation of the weights gives sub-Gaussian concentration under conditions. [abstract] — [Metelli, Russo, Restelli, 2021, NeurIPS 34, "Subgaussian and Differentiable Importance Sampling for Off-Policy Evaluation and Learning"](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/4476b929e30dd0c4e8bdbcc82c6ba23a-Abstract.html)

**Marginalised / embedding estimators (MIPS, OffCEM)**
- IPS variance and MSE "inflate by a factor of over 300 when the number of actions increases from 10 to 5000 given a fixed sample size". MIPS weights on action embeddings and is unbiased under common embedding support plus "no direct effect" of the action given its embedding; violating the latter gives a characterised bias. [full text, partial] — [Saito, Joachims, 2022, ICML, "Off-Policy Evaluation for Large Action Spaces via Embeddings"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06317)
- OffCEM applies importance weighting only to action clusters and models the residual effect; unbiased under "local correctness". [abstract] — [Saito, Ren, Joachims, 2023, ICML, "Off-Policy Evaluation for Large Action Spaces via Conjunct Effect Modeling"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08062)

**Estimator selection and tuning**
- SLOPE (Lepski's principle) orders candidate estimators by increasing bias and decreasing confidence width, and picks the largest index whose interval still intersects all previous ones; it satisfies an oracle inequality with constant 6(1 + 1/kappa). The authors note the implied MSE bound "is typically loose in constant factors". [full text, partial] — [Su, Srinath, Krishnamurthy, 2020, ICML, "Adaptive Estimator Selection for Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07729)
- No single estimator dominates; accuracy varies with evaluation policy, number of actions and noise. Selection by subsampling the log into pseudo logging/evaluation policies improved estimator choice. [abstract] — [Udagawa, Kiyohara, Narita, Saito, Tateno, 2023, AAAI, "Policy-Adaptive Estimator Selection for Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13904)
- Robustness of estimators to hyperparameters and evaluation policies should be tested (IEOE procedure). [abstract] — [Saito, Udagawa, Kiyohara, Mogi, Narita, Tateno, 2021, RecSys, "Evaluating the Robustness of Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13703)
- Cross-validation can be used for estimator selection and tuning in OPE. [abstract] — [Cief, Kveton, Kompan, 2024, arXiv (to appear AAAI 2025), "Cross-Validated Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15332). A supervised meta-model for estimator selection: [abstract] — [Felicioni, Benigni, Ferrari Dacrema, 2024, arXiv, "Automated Off-Policy Estimator Selection via Supervised Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18022)

**Software**
- Open Bandit Pipeline's estimator page documents DM, IPW, SNIPW, DR, SNDR, Switch-DR, MRDR and DRos; it states DM's misspecification "cannot be easily quantified from data". [docs] — [Open Bandit Pipeline documentation](https://zr-obp.readthedocs.io/en/latest/estimators.html)
- Vowpal Wabbit's tutorial recommends IPS or DR for evaluation ("If you are unsure, we suggest using dr"), says "the use of dm is discouraged for OPE since it is biased", and discourages the legacy `--eval` option. [docs, version 9.6.0] — [Vowpal Wabbit, "Offline Policy Evaluation Using the Command Line"](https://vowpalwabbit.org/docs/vowpal_wabbit/python/9.6.0/tutorials/off_policy_evaluation.html)

### Inferences
- With known propensities bounded below (epsilon-greedy), weights are bounded, so the problem is variance, not infinite variance. The irreducible term E_mu[rho^2 sigma^2] means the only large lever inside the estimator is a good reward model (DR residuals); Switch/DRos/clipping then trade the remaining large-weight variance for model bias.
- Ranking for a few-thousand-decision log with 5 to 8 actions: (1) DR / SNDR with a cross-fitted reward model as the unbiased or nearly unbiased reference; (2) DRos or Switch-DR with data-driven tuning as the low-variance candidate, reported alongside (1) because their bias cannot be bounded from the data; (3) DM as a sanity check only. The OBP small-sample result (DM best at n = 10,000) shows the low-variance estimators can win on RMSE while giving no valid interval.
- MIPS and OffCEM are unlikely to help at 5 to 8 actions unless the actions share an embedding that fully mediates their effect; the reported gains are for hundreds to thousands of actions.
- An interval that must exclude zero needs a known-bias-free estimator or an explicit bias bound; shrinkage estimators tuned on the same data do not supply one.

### Gaps
- No source gives head-to-head results at n of about 1,000 to 5,000 with 5 to 8 actions; the smallest benchmark sample size I found stated was n = 10,000 (OBP). The UCI experiments in Wang et al. and Su et al. include smaller datasets but I did not extract per-dataset sizes.
- I could not open the DRos arXiv PDF (size limit) and read only the first 100,000 characters of the ar5iv HTML; the lambda grid and the appendix were not read.
- Li, Munos, Szepesvari (2015): abstract only; which two estimators are "arbitrarily worse" is named only via Wang et al.
- OBP "latest" docs page lists eight estimators; the code may contain more (the page may be outdated).

## 2. Reward-to-go within a session: sequential estimators, when the session must be an MDP episode, and what goes wrong with per-decision bandit OPE

### Takeaway
If an action changes what happens later in the session, the value of the learned policy is a sequential quantity, and per-decision bandit OPE with logged reward-to-go estimates something else: the value of deviating at one step and then following the logging policy. Unbiased sequential importance sampling pays weights that multiply along the session, so at this data size the workable sequential options are model-based ones (FQE, then DR/WDR built on it), whose bias cannot be certified from the data.

### Cited Findings

**How bandit and sequential estimators relate**
- "Contextual bandits may be considered as MDPs with horizon 1." Step-wise IS can be viewed as a bandit problem at each step t whose "observed stochastic return" is r_t + gamma * V_step-IS of the remaining steps, i.e. the future return is itself importance-corrected. Trajectory-wise IS is rho_{1:H} * sum of rewards; step-wise IS is the sum over t of rho_{1:t} r_t; both use the cumulative ratio rho_{1:t}. "IS (even the step-wise version) suffers from very high variance, which easily grows exponentially in horizon." [full text] — [Jiang, Li, 2016, ICML, "Doubly Robust Off-policy Value Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03722)
- Replacing the full product of weights by the current-step weight only is a "first-order approximation" that "ignore[s] the state visitation differences under the two policies" and gives "a slightly biased estimator ... with lower variance"; the authors cite Achiam et al. (2017) for a bound on the effect of this approximation. [full text, partial] — [Chen, Beutel, Covington, Jain, Belletti, Chi, 2018, arXiv (WSDM 2019, venue not on record), "Top-K Off-Policy Correction for a REINFORCE Recommender System"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02353); bound source [abstract only, bound not read] — [Achiam, Held, Tamar, Abbeel, 2017, ICML, "Constrained Policy Optimization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.10528)
- One step of constrained policy improvement using an on-policy Q estimate of the behaviour policy performs well and is more robust than iterative methods, whose weakness is attributed to "the high variance inherent in doing off-policy evaluation". [abstract] — [Brandfonbrener, Whitney, Ranganath, Bruna, 2021, NeurIPS, "Offline RL Without Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08909)
- Myopic (bandit) methods and their click-through metric are not the right objective or evaluation for policies meant to optimise value over a user's whole trajectory; the authors evaluate life-time value with trajectory-level high-confidence OPE (concentration inequality, t-test, bootstrap) and report results "with a relatively small data set". [full text, partial] — [Theocharous, Thomas, Ghavamzadeh, 2015, IJCAI (venue not printed in the copy read), "Personalized Ad Recommendation Systems for Life-Time Value Optimization with Guarantees"](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~pthomas/papers/Theocharous2015a.pdf)

**Curse of horizon and when it is avoidable**
- Step-wise methods' MSE "grows exponentially fast with respect to the horizon"; this "is unavoidable when the observed data are generated by a non-Markov decision process (NMDP)" but can be avoided under Markov structure. The NMDP efficiency bound contains squared cumulative ratios lambda_t^2; the time-varying-MDP bound contains squared marginal ratios mu_t^2. Contextual bandits are the H = 1 special case. Step-wise methods are also "inconsistent with finitely many trajectories". [full text, partial] — [Uehara, Shi, Kallus, 2022, arXiv, "A Review of Off-Policy Evaluation in Reinforcement Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06355)
- For discrete tree MDPs the variance of any unbiased estimator is at least the sum over t of E[rho_{1:(t-1)}^2 Var_t(V(s_t))]; DR with a perfect Q attains it. DR "only reduces the variance due to action stochasticity"; variance from stochastic transitions and rewards remains, amplified by cumulative weights. [full text] — [Jiang, Li, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03722)
- IS variance can be unbounded in infinite-horizon problems; applying IS to stationary state-visitation distributions avoids this. [abstract] — [Liu, Li, Tang, Zhou, 2018, NeurIPS, "Breaking the Curse of Horizon"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12429). Marginalised IS replaces the exponential dependence on H by marginal state ratios. [abstract] — [Xie, Ma, Wang, 2019, NeurIPS, "Towards Optimal Off-Policy Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning with Marginalized Importance Sampling"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03393)
- With a deterministic target policy, "the number of sequences with non-zero weights decays exponentially with the length of sequences"; in a sepsis cohort of 3,855 test patients only 25 on average had non-zero weight, and 167 for the deterministic policy closest to clinicians. [full text, partial] — [Gottesman et al., 2018, arXiv, "Evaluating Reinforcement Learning Algorithms in Observational Health Settings"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12298)

**Estimator families and what an empirical benchmark recommends**
- Definitions and properties: IS and per-decision IS are unbiased; weighted IS and consistent weighted per-decision IS are biased but consistent; weighted doubly robust (WDR) is biased, strongly consistent, and "dominated the other (guided) importance sampling estimators, sometimes achieving orders of magnitude lower MSE"; MAGIC blends model-based and WDR j-step returns to minimise estimated MSE (bias from a confidence interval on WDR, covariance by bootstrap). If rewards or transitions are stochastic and weights have high variance, DR and WDR can still have high variance. [full text, partial] — [Thomas, Brunskill, 2016, arXiv (ICML 2016, venue not on record), "Data-Efficient Off-Policy Policy Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00923)
- Benchmark guidance: "no single method or method class is consistently the best". Among IPS methods per-decision weighted IS tends to be best. "FQE tends to be more data efficient and is the best method when data is limited"; "FQE and IH are best in the small data regime". "DM frequently outperform their DR versions in the small data case"; hybrids give consistent gains "when data is sufficient, or model misspecification is severe". WDR usually beats DR, and MAGIC often beats both but adds hyperparameters. Policy mismatch can matter more than horizon: lengthening the horizon gave about 10x worse accuracy, a comparable increase in policy divergence about 100x. Model-based (transition-model) direct estimation is among the worst with function approximation. [full text, partial] — [Voloshin, Le, Jiang, Yue, 2019 (v3 2021), arXiv (NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks 2021, venue not on record), "Empirical Study of Off-Policy Policy Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06854)
- FQE was introduced as a simple OPE method with PAC-style bounds. [abstract] — [Le, Voloshin, Yue, 2019, arXiv (ICML 2019, venue not on record), "Batch Policy Learning under Constraints"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08738)
- Double reinforcement learning combines cross-fitted Q-functions and marginal density ratios; efficient when both are estimated at fourth-root rates and doubly robust when one is consistent. [abstract] — [Kallus, Uehara, 2020, arXiv (JMLR, venue not on record), "Double Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Off-Policy Evaluation in Markov Decision Processes"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08526)
- DualDICE estimates discounted stationary distribution corrections without knowing the behaviour policy and without explicit importance weights. [abstract] — [Nachum, Chow, Dai, Li, 2019, NeurIPS, "DualDICE"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04733). The DICE family is unified as regularised Lagrangians of one linear program. [abstract] — [Yang, Nachum, Dai, Li, Schuurmans, 2020, arXiv, "Off-Policy Evaluation via the Regularized Lagrangian"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.03438)
- SCOPE-RL implements DM/FQE, trajectory-wise and per-decision IS, DR, self-normalised versions, state- and state-action-marginal IS/DR, double RL and a spectrum between per-decision and marginal weights; it notes per-decision weights "can be exponentially large in the latter part of the trajectory" and that marginal-weight estimation error adds bias. [docs] — [SCOPE-RL documentation](https://scope-rl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/evaluation_implementation.html); paper [abstract] — [Kiyohara, Kishimoto, Kawakami, Kobayashi, Nakata, Saito, 2023, arXiv, "SCOPE-RL"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18206)

### Inferences
- **What per-decision bandit OPE with reward-to-go estimates.** Treating each decision as a bandit round with the logged reward-to-go as its reward estimates, averaged over logged states, Q^mu(s, pi(s)): take the target's action once, then follow the logging policy. That is the "first-order approximation" of Chen et al. and the one-step quantity of Brandfonbrener et al. It equals the value of running the target policy for the whole session only if later states and rewards do not depend on the current action, or the target differs from the logging policy at one step. It is a legitimate estimand for a one-step improvement claim, not for "the learned policy acts throughout the session".
- **Two further defects of the per-decision treatment.** (a) Reward-to-go values of decisions in one session overlap, so decisions are positively correlated and any interval treating them as independent is too narrow (design effect of the form 1 + (M - 1)rho, see section 3). (b) Averaging reward-to-go over decisions weights sessions by their length and counts late rewards once per earlier decision; the per-decision mean is not the per-session value.
- **When the session must be an episode.** Treat it as an MDP/POMDP episode whenever actions can change the session's continuation (length, later contexts, later rewards). A direct check is whether logged next-state or session-length distributions differ by action given context.
- **Feasibility of unbiased sequential IS here (my arithmetic, assuming epsilon-greedy with epsilon = 0.3, K = 5, a deterministic target disagreeing with the rule on a fraction d of steps independently, and about 5 decisions per session).** Per-step E[w^2] = (1 - d)/0.76 + d/0.06, i.e. 2.85 at d = 0.1 and 5.92 at d = 0.3. Over 5 steps the trajectory weight's second moment is about 188 and 7,300, so 950 sessions give a trajectory-IS effective sample size of about 5 and below 1. Per-decision IS helps only the early steps. Unbiased sequential evaluation of a policy that departs from the rule on more than a few percent of steps is out of reach at this size.
- **Practical choice.** For a sequential claim: FQE with a simple function class (the benchmark's best method with limited data), with WDR/DR on top as a consistency check, accepting that FQE bias is uncertifiable. If the session state can be made Markov (a compact summary of history), marginalised estimators avoid cumulative weights in principle, but they need density-ratio models fitted on about 5,000 transitions; I found no evidence they are reliable at that size.

### Gaps
- No source I opened analyses exactly "bandit OPE applied to within-episode reward-to-go"; the characterisation above is assembled from Jiang and Li, Chen et al., and Brandfonbrener et al. The quantitative bound on the first-order approximation (Achiam et al., 2017) was not read.
- Precup, Sutton, Singh (2000, ICML, "Eligibility Traces for Off-Policy Policy Evaluation"), the origin of per-decision and weighted IS, was **not opened**; it is reported via Thomas and Brunskill (2016) and Jiang and Li (2016).
- No source gives minimum data sizes for DualDICE-family or double-RL estimators; the benchmark papers use larger or simulated datasets.
- Voloshin et al.'s decision tree (their Figure 2) was read only through the surrounding text, not the figure.

## 3. Confidence intervals and high-confidence guarantees: which are valid with dependent decisions and heavy-tailed weights?

### Takeaway
Every method with a finite-sample guarantee requires independent units or a martingale mean condition, so the unit must be the session, not the decision; resampling or bounding at session level is the correct treatment of dependence. Exact concentration bounds are valid under heavy tails but needed hundreds to millions of trajectories in the original papers; Student-t and BCa bootstrap intervals are approximate and can exceed their nominal error rate with skewed weights and few effective sessions; betting-based intervals are the tightest exact option reported.

### Cited Findings

**Exact (finite-sample) bounds**
- HCOPE's Theorem 1 gives a lower bound for the mean of n independent (not necessarily identically distributed) random variables bounded below, using per-variable truncation thresholds c_i and the Maurer-Pontil empirical Bernstein inequality; the random variables are per-trajectory importance-weighted returns, which have "a very long tail" (in their mountain-car example the upper bound on the weighted return is about 10^9.4 while the largest observed value was about 316). The threshold is tuned on 1/20 of the data and the bound computed on the other 19/20. In the digital-marketing simulator, improvement over the behaviour policy (0.0765 versus true 0.086) was guaranteed at 95% "using only 2 million trajectories" (bound 0.077; 0.0803 with 5 million); Chernoff-Hoeffding "never achieved a lower bound above -22". [full text] — [Thomas, Theocharous, Ghavamzadeh, 2015, AAAI, "High Confidence Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~pthomas/papers/Thomas2015.pdf) (record: [AAAI OJS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9541))
- Empirical Bernstein bounds are variance-sensitive and data-dependent. [abstract] — [Maurer, Pontil, 2009, COLT, "Empirical Bernstein Bounds and Sample Variance Penalization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.3740)
- For DR on i.i.d. trajectories "standard concentration bounds apply" (e.g. Hoeffding with the estimate's range), but "strict CIs are usually too pessimistic, and normal approximations are used instead". [full text] — [Jiang, Li, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03722). Exact bounds "often require an impractical amount of data before they are tight enough to be useful", and they need an unbiased estimator, which rules out WIS, WDR and MAGIC. [full text] — [Thomas, Brunskill, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00923)
- A self-normalised-IS lower bound built on a semi-empirical Efron-Stein inequality with multiplicative bias control; assumes independent context-action-reward triples, rewards in [0,1] and a known behaviour policy; reported tighter than competitors. Its variance proxy is O(1/n) when policies coincide and Theta(1) under full mismatch, tracking effective sample size. [full text, partial] — [Kuzborskij, Vernade, Gyorgy, Szepesvari, 2020 (v3 2022), arXiv (AISTATS 2021, venue not on record), "Confident Off-Policy Evaluation and Selection through Self-Normalized Importance Weighting"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10460)
- A Cantelli-type bound: with probability 1 - delta, E_P[f] >= IS estimate - ||f||_inf * sqrt((1 - delta) d_2(P||Q) / (delta N)), where d_2 is the exponentiated 2-Renyi divergence; requires i.i.d. samples and finite d_2. [full text] — [Metelli, Papini, Faccio, Restelli, 2018, NeurIPS, "Policy Optimization via Importance Sampling"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06098)

**Betting-based confidence sequences and intervals**
- Betting-based bounds for bounded means adapt to unknown variance and "empirically vastly outperform" Hoeffding and empirical Bernstein approaches. [abstract] — [Waudby-Smith, Ramdas, 2020 (v7), arXiv (JRSS-B, venue not on record), "Estimating means of bounded random variables by betting"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09686)
- Off-policy confidence sequences for contextual bandits: non-asymptotic, non-parametric, valid at arbitrary stopping times; the setting is i.i.d. (x, a, r) data from a historical policy; a reward predictor can be added as a control variate; applied to "gated deployment" (deploy once the sequence shows the new policy is better). Empirically the sequences approach nominal coverage from above. Some constructions require a known w_max. [full text, partial] — [Karampatziakis, Mineiro, Ramdas, 2021, arXiv (ICML 2021, venue not on record), "Off-policy Confidence Sequences"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09540)
- A follow-up satisfies five properties: non-asymptotic, non-parametric, time-uniform, valid when logging policies are predictable (adaptive), and not needing w_max to be known or finite; widths "depend only on empirical variances". Rewards must lie in [0,1]. The i.i.d. assumption is relaxed to a conditional-mean condition E_pi(R_t | H_{t-1}) = nu, and a separate section gives sequences for a time-varying policy value. Fixed-time versions exist; in the authors' simulation the betting CI beat the closed-form CI, "which itself significantly outperforms the bounds in Thomas et al.", with the caveat that the comparison "is by no means comprehensive". They note non-asymptotic procedures may be more conservative than asymptotic ones. [full text, partial] — [Waudby-Smith, Wu, Ramdas, Karampatziakis, Mineiro, 2022 (v3), arXiv, "Anytime-valid off-policy inference for contextual bandits"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10768)

**Approximate intervals: Student-t, BCa, empirical likelihood, bootstrap**
- Importance-weighted returns have heavy upper tails, which "can make the t-test overly conservative even for seemingly large data sets", yet "in some cases it may still result in an error rate greater than delta". BCa (2,000 resamples) "can correct for the heavy tails" but "may have error rates larger than delta". In their Figure 1 (heavy-upper-tail distribution, 100,000 trials) the concentration bound had zero errors, the t-test started conservative and approached 5%, BCa stayed near 5%. Methods using t-test or BCa are called "semi-safe". On Mountain Car the exact bound needed 900 trajectories for safe improvement (400 with k-fold candidate selection); t-test needed less and BCa least. [full text] — [Thomas, Theocharous, Ghavamzadeh, 2015, ICML (JMLR W&CP 37), "High Confidence Policy Improvement"](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~pthomas/papers/Thomas2015b.pdf)
- Bootstrap bounds "only approximate" the allowed error rate and "all methods can do worse than 5% when data is extremely sparse". Model-based and WDR bootstraps gave tight bounds with low error when the model was good; when model error is high, IS- or PDIS-based bootstrapping "provides the safest approximate" method; returns should be normalised; where safety must be strictly guaranteed, concentration inequalities with IS are indicated. [full text, partial] — [Hanna, Stone, Niekum, 2017, AAMAS, "Bootstrapping with Models: Confidence Intervals for Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06126)
- When weight variance is "merely very large, the central limit convergence might occur too slowly to justify" CLT intervals; when infinite the CLT fails. [full text] — [Bottou et al., 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.2355)
- An empirical-likelihood estimator and interval for contextual-bandit OPE: only asymptotically valid, assumes w in [w_min, w_max], allows the logging policy to depend on past data; empirically approaches nominal coverage from above, is "dramatically tighter" than a binomial interval, and avoids the "chronic undercoverage" of the asymptotic Gaussian interval. [full text, partial] — [Karampatziakis, Langford, Mineiro, 2019/2020, NeurIPS 2020, "Empirical Likelihood for Contextual Bandits"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03323)
- SCOPE-RL offers Hoeffding, empirical Bernstein, Student-t and bootstrap bounds; the first two make no distributional assumption and can be "quite conservative"; the t-test assumes normality. [docs] — [SCOPE-RL documentation](https://scope-rl.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation/evaluation_implementation.html)

**Dependence within clusters (sessions)**
- The pairs cluster bootstrap resamples whole clusters with equal probability and is "the only procedure applicable to every model that uses clustered data". "There is no magic number" of clusters: inference "can be fairly reliable when G = 20, but in unfavorable [cases] it can be unreliable even when G = 200 or more"; it fails when one or a few clusters are unusually large or have high leverage. With constant cluster size M and intra-cluster correlation rho_s, ignoring clustering understates variance by the Moulton factor 1 + (M - 1) rho_s. Investigators should inspect the bootstrap distributions. [full text, partial] — [MacKinnon, Nielsen, Webb, 2022, arXiv (Journal of Econometrics, accepted), "Cluster-Robust Inference: A Guide to Empirical Practice"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03285)
- Clustering adjustments are a design question (how units were sampled and assigned), and conventional clustering can be conservative. [abstract] — [Abadie, Athey, Imbens, Wooldridge, 2017, arXiv (QJE, venue not on record), "When Should You Adjust Standard Errors for Clustering?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02926)

**Intervals for model-based / marginalised estimators**
- Bootstrapping FQE is asymptotically efficient and distributionally consistent for off-policy inference. [abstract] — [Hao, Ji, Duan, Lu, Szepesvari, Wang, 2021, ICML, "Bootstrapping Fitted Q-Evaluation for Off-Policy Inference"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03607)
- CoinDICE gives behaviour-agnostic intervals via generalised empirical likelihood, valid asymptotically and in finite samples under its function-class assumptions. [abstract] — [Dai, Nachum, Chow, Li, Szepesvari, Schuurmans, 2020, NeurIPS, "CoinDICE"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11652)
- A minimax value interval is valid when either the value-function class or the weight class is well specified, quantifying function-approximation bias. [abstract] — [Jiang, Huang, 2020, arXiv, "Minimax Value Interval for Off-Policy Evaluation and Policy Optimization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02081)
- Further interval methods for Markov settings: [abstract] — [Shi, Zhang, Lu, Song, 2020, arXiv, "Statistical Inference of the Value Function for Reinforcement Learning in Infinite Horizon Settings"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04515); [abstract] — [Shi, Wan, Chernozhukov, Song, 2021, arXiv, "Deeply-Debiased Off-Policy Interval Estimation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04646). High-confidence bounds for any parameter of the return distribution: [abstract] — [Chandak, Niekum, da Silva, Learned-Miller, Brunskill, Thomas, 2021, NeurIPS, "Universal Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12820)

### Inferences
- **Validity under within-session dependence.** HCOPE, Hoeffding/Bernstein, Efron-Stein and Cantelli bounds all assume independent summands; decisions in one session sharing reward-to-go violate this. Collapsing each session to one number (the session's summed estimator contribution) restores independence across sessions. The session bootstrap already in use is the same idea and is the right resampling unit.
- **Betting sequences at decision level.** The relaxed condition in Waudby-Smith et al. is a constant conditional mean given the past; per-decision reward-to-go within a session will not generally satisfy it (the conditional mean depends on position and state in the session), so the safe use is on session-level, [0,1]-normalised quantities, or their time-varying-value variant, which I did not read in detail. Either way the target is still the one-step-deviation value unless session-level weights are used.
- **Why the current bootstrap is fragile.** 950 sessions is a comfortable number of clusters by itself, but with weights up to 16.7 per decision (26.7 at eight actions) a handful of sessions carry most of the weight; that is the "few high-leverage clusters" case where MacKinnon et al. say inference fails, and the "extremely sparse" case where Hanna et al. and Thomas et al. say bootstrap and BCa can exceed nominal error. Percentile or BCa session bootstrap at an effective sample size of 90 to 200 should be treated as approximate, possibly anti-conservative.
- **A defensible stack for the decision rule "interval excludes zero".** (1) Primary: betting-based fixed-time CI (or CS if the data will be monitored as they arrive) on per-session DR contributions of the difference (learned minus rule), rescaled to [0,1]; exact under independence of sessions and bounded rewards. (2) Secondary: empirical-likelihood interval and session-level BCa bootstrap as tighter approximate checks. (3) Report the exact and approximate intervals side by side; disagreement between them is itself a diagnostic.
- Evaluating the difference directly (one summand per session for learned minus rule) is tighter than differencing two intervals because the two estimates share data; decisions where both policies choose the same action contribute exactly zero.
- Intervals for FQE or DICE estimators (bootstrapped FQE, CoinDICE) cover sampling error under a correct function class; they do not cover misspecification bias, so they cannot by themselves support a deployment gate.

### Gaps
- No source evaluates coverage of session-level bootstrap or BCa intervals specifically for importance-weighted estimators at effective sample sizes near 100 to 200; the HCPI and Hanna et al. simulations are the nearest evidence.
- I found no paper that derives OPE confidence intervals for clustered contextual-bandit data as such; the cluster guidance comes from econometrics (MacKinnon et al.), where the estimator is a regression coefficient.
- Efron (1987, JASA, BCa) and Cameron, Gelbach, Miller (2008, Review of Economics and Statistics, bootstrap with few clusters) were **not opened**; reported via Thomas et al. (2015) and MacKinnon et al. (2022).
- The betting-CI comparisons I read are the authors' own simulations; I did not find an independent benchmark of betting versus bootstrap intervals for OPE.

## 4. Safe policy improvement and conservative learning: what is sensible with a few thousand decisions?

### Takeaway
At a few thousand decisions the sensible learners are those that stay near the logging rule and optimise a pessimistic or variance-penalised objective over a small policy class: SPIBB-style baseline bootstrapping, counterfactual risk minimisation with self-normalisation, and PAC-Bayes or lower-confidence-bound objectives. Deep offline RL methods (CQL, IQL, BCQ) are documented as hyperparameter-sensitive and were developed on much larger datasets, and provable SPIBB guarantees need far more data than is available.

### Cited Findings

**SPIBB family**
- SPIBB keeps the baseline policy's probabilities on state-action pairs seen fewer than N_wedge times and optimises elsewhere; Pi_b-SPIBB has a guarantee, Pi_<=b-SPIBB is a heuristic that is "even more efficient in practice". The guarantee's error term scales as (4 Vmax / (1 - gamma)) * sqrt((2 / N_wedge) * log(2|X||A|2^|X| / delta)). N_wedge "is not a property of the dataset, but a hyper-parameter"; experiments used N_wedge of 5 to 20 on datasets from 10 trajectories upward; "the bigger the N_wedge, the more conservative SPIBB gets". Benchmarked against Basic RL, HCPI, Robust MDP and reward-adjusted MDP using mean and 1%-CVaR performance. The method needs the baseline policy's action probabilities. [full text, partial] — [Laroche, Trichelair, Tachet des Combes, 2019, ICML, "Safe Policy Improvement with Baseline Bootstrapping"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.06924)
- Soft-SPIBB constrains policy change in proportion to local uncertainty. [abstract] — [Nadjahi, Laroche, Tachet des Combes, 2019, ECML-PKDD, "Safe Policy Improvement with Soft Baseline Bootstrapping"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05079). Its safety claim "does not hold"; provably safe variants exist, a heuristic variant performs best empirically, and "for all algorithms a huge amount of data is necessary to get a sharp bound and produce a good policy at the same time" (one competitor only reproduced the baseline until 50,000 steps and became useful at 500,000). [full text, partial] — [Scholl, Dietrich, Otte, Udluft, 2022, arXiv, "Safe Policy Improvement Approaches and their Limitations"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00724)
- Tighter SPI guarantees that need less data for the same guarantee. [abstract] — [Wienhoft, Suilen, Simao, Dubslaff, Baier, Jansen, 2023, IJCAI, "More for Less: Safe Policy Improvement With Stronger Performance Guarantees"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07958)

**High-confidence policy improvement (HCPI)**
- Split the data (1/5 to choose a candidate, 4/5 for the safety test), return the candidate only if its lower bound beats the threshold, else "No Solution Found"; without a held-out test the method "has a tendency to overfit" when trajectories are few. [full text] — [Thomas, Theocharous, Ghavamzadeh, 2015, ICML](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~pthomas/papers/Thomas2015b.pdf). DR with normal-approximation intervals gave "more effective and reliable policy improvement than IS" in that framework. [full text] — [Jiang, Li, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03722)

**Counterfactual risk minimisation and successors (contextual bandits)**
- CRM minimises clipped IPS risk plus lambda times an empirical-variance penalty; POEM implements it for stochastic linear policies. Hyperparameters (clipping M, lambda) are chosen by validation, with 25% of the data held out in the experiments. [full text, partial] — [Swaminathan, Joachims, 2015, arXiv (ICML 2015, venue not on record), "Counterfactual Risk Minimization: Learning from Logged Bandit Feedback"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.02362)
- Self-normalisation (Norm-POEM) avoids propensity overfitting. [abstract] — [Swaminathan, Joachims, 2015, NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2015/hash/39027dfad5138c9ca0c474d71db915c3-Abstract.html). BanditNet reformulates the self-normalised objective with a Lagrange multiplier (a loss baseline) so deep networks can be trained by SGD; motivated by logs available "in huge quantities". [full text, partial] — [Joachims, Swaminathan, de Rijke, 2018, ICLR, "Deep Learning with Logged Bandit Feedback"](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/people/tj/publications/joachims_etal_18a.pdf)
- PAC-Bayes: a bound for truncated IPS motivating a regulariser toward the logging policy, "competitive with variance regularization while being both simpler". [abstract] — [London, Sandler, 2019, ICML, "Bayesian Counterfactual Risk Minimization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.11500). Tighter bounds that can be optimised directly "to confidently improve upon the logging policy offline", using all data with no held-out tuning. [abstract] — [Sakhi, Alquier, Chopin, 2023, ICML, "PAC-Bayesian Offline Contextual Bandits With Guarantees"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13132). A two-sided bound valid for unclipped IPS that identifies cases where regularisation is not needed. [abstract] — [Aouali, Brunel, Rohde, Korba, 2023, ICML, "Exponential Smoothing for Off-Policy Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15877). Fully empirical pessimistic bounds for a class of weighted estimators and a logarithmic-smoothing estimator. [abstract] — [Sakhi, Aouali, Alquier, Chopin, 2024, NeurIPS, "Logarithmic Smoothing for Pessimistic Off-Policy Evaluation, Selection and Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14335)
- Pessimism: optimise lower confidence bounds built from known behaviour propensities, with guarantees depending only on overlap for the optimal policy and policy-class complexity. [abstract] — [Jin, Ren, Yang, Wang, 2022, arXiv (to appear, Annals of Statistics), "Policy learning 'without' overlap"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09900). A general oracle-efficient pessimistic method reducing to supervised learning. [abstract] — [Wang, Krishnamurthy, Slivkins, 2023, arXiv, "Oracle-Efficient Pessimism"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07923). The implicit-exploration estimator gives better guarantees than pessimistic adjustment of IPS. [abstract] — [Gabbianelli, Neu, Papini, 2023, arXiv, "Importance-Weighted Offline Learning Done Right"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15771). Optimising an empirical-likelihood lower bound outperformed a strong off-policy baseline. [abstract] — [Karampatziakis, Langford, Mineiro, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03323)
- Policy learning with doubly robust scores over restricted classes such as decision trees, with asymptotically minimax-optimal regret for multiple actions. [abstract] — [Athey, Wager, 2017 (v6), arXiv (Econometrica), "Policy Learning with Observational Data"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02896); [Zhou, Athey, Wager, 2018, arXiv (Operations Research, venue not on record), "Offline Multi-Action Policy Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04778)
- Support-deficient logs break IPS learning; remedies are restricting the action space, reward extrapolation, or restricting the policy space. [abstract] — [Sachdeva, Su, Joachims, 2020, KDD, "Off-policy Bandits with Deficient Support"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09438)

**Offline deep RL (CQL, IQL, BCQ) and small data**
- CQL learns a Q-function whose policy value lower-bounds the true value. [abstract] — [Kumar, Zhou, Tucker, Levine, 2020, arXiv (NeurIPS 2020, venue not on record), "Conservative Q-Learning for Offline Reinforcement Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04779). IQL never queries out-of-dataset actions, using expectile regression. [abstract] — [Kostrikov, Nair, Levine, 2021, arXiv, "Offline Reinforcement Learning with Implicit Q-Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06169). BCQ restricts actions to those close to the batch. [abstract] — [Fujimoto, Meger, Precup, 2019, ICML, "Off-Policy Deep Reinforcement Learning without Exploration"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02900). Adding a behaviour-cloning term to TD3 matches more complex methods. [abstract] — [Fujimoto, Gu, 2021, NeurIPS, "A Minimalist Approach to Offline Reinforcement Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06860). Pessimistic value iteration is minimax optimal in linear MDPs. [abstract] — [Jin, Yang, Wang, 2020, arXiv, "Is Pessimism Provably Efficient for Offline RL?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15085)
- "Offline RL algorithms are not robust to hyperparameter choices." [abstract] — [Paine, Paduraru, Michi, Gulcehre, Zolna, Novikov, Wang, de Freitas, 2020, arXiv, "Hyperparameter Selection for Offline Reinforcement Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09055)
- With limited data (their examples range from a few hundred trajectories to a 14,000-patient cohort; one experiment uses N = 1,000), a single train/validation split is unreliable: policy performance and its WIS/FQE estimate vary widely with the split. Their pipeline uses repeated random sub-sampling splits to select algorithm and hyperparameters, then retrains on all data. [full text, partial] — [Nie, Flet-Berliac, Jordan, Steenbergen, Brunskill, 2022, NeurIPS, "Data-Efficient Pipeline for Offline Reinforcement Learning with Limited Data"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08642)
- One-step improvement on the behaviour policy's Q-function is simpler and more robust to hyperparameters than iterative offline RL. [abstract] — [Brandfonbrener et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08909). Conditions under which offline RL beats behavioural cloning: [abstract] — [Kumar, Hong, Singh, Levine, 2022, ICLR, "When Should We Prefer Offline Reinforcement Learning Over Behavioral Cloning?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05618)
- Using an unbiased OPE estimate as the objective for hyperparameter search can select hyperparameters "whose generalization performance is greatly overestimated". [abstract] — [Saito, Nomura, 2024, IJCAI, "Hyperparameter Optimization Can Even be Harmful in Off-Policy Learning and How to Deal with It"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15084)

### Inferences
- The logging policy here is known exactly and is itself "rule plus exploration", which is the setting SPIBB, CRM and the PAC-Bayes/pessimistic learners assume; that is an advantage over the usual observational case.
- A learner that changes the rule's action only where the data support it also helps evaluation: the fewer decisions on which the candidate disagrees with the rule, the smaller the weights and the larger the effective sample size (section 5). Conservative learning and evaluability pull in the same direction.
- Reasonable shortlist at this size: (a) a one-step improvement on a fitted Q of the logging policy, restricted to deviate only where state-action counts or a lower confidence bound allow (SPIBB-style); (b) a low-capacity stochastic policy trained with a self-normalised, variance-penalised or PAC-Bayes objective; (c) a shallow policy tree on doubly robust scores. CQL, IQL and BCQ add many hyperparameters for which there is no reliable offline selection at this size; I would not make them the first choice.
- SPIBB's formal guarantee is not attainable here (the theoretical N_wedge is far above what works in practice, and Scholl et al. needed 10^4 to 10^5+ steps); use N_wedge as a conservatism dial and rely on the held-out OPE interval for the actual gate.
- Whatever the learner, the candidate must be fixed before the evaluation data are looked at (HCPI's split; Jiang and Li's independence requirement), and hyperparameter choice should use repeated splits (Nie et al.) inside the training portion only.

### Gaps
- None of the offline-RL papers reports results at about 5,000 transitions with five to eight discrete actions; the claim that deep offline RL is a poor fit at this size is my inference from the hyperparameter-sensitivity and small-data findings, not a published result.
- SPIBB is defined for finite MDPs with counts; for continuous contexts it needs pseudo-counts (the paper's SPIBB-DQN). I did not read that section in detail.
- CQL, IQL, BCQ, TD3+BC, PAC-Bayes and pessimism papers were read at abstract level only.

## 5. Sample-size planning: effective sample size versus epsilon, number of actions and policy departure; rules of thumb; policy-aware logging

### Takeaway
For a deterministic target policy under epsilon-greedy logging, the effective sample size per logged decision is roughly epsilon / (d * K) once the target disagrees with the rule on a fraction d of decisions, so the data requirement grows in proportion to K * d / epsilon; published formulas give the same scaling but no universal threshold. Logging policies that place probability on the candidate's own actions (or a uniform slice, or a session-level randomised trial) reduce the requirement far more than collecting more data under the same logging.

### Cited Findings
- Effective sample size: ESS(P||Q) = N / d_2(P||Q) = N / (Var_Q[w] + 1), estimated by (sum w)^2 / sum w^2 (equivalently 1 / sum of squared normalised weights). IS variance satisfies Var <= ||f||_inf^2 d_2(P||Q) / N = ||f||_inf^2 / ESS, "i.e., the variance scales with ESS instead of N". [full text] — [Metelli, Papini, Faccio, Restelli, 2018, NeurIPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06098)
- n_e = (sum w_i)^2 / sum w_i^2; "the point at which n_e becomes alarmingly small is hard to specify, because it is application specific". "Effective sample sizes are imperfect diagnostics": too small signals a problem, large does not prove success; a separate, smaller effective sample size governs the variance estimate. Defensive importance sampling mixes the nominal distribution into the sampling distribution to bound the likelihood ratio. [full text, partial] — [Owen, "Monte Carlo theory, methods and examples", Chapter 9 "Importance sampling"](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/Ch-var-is.pdf)
- The usual ESS approximation rests on several assumptions and approximations and is hard to regard as a reasonable approximation of the true ESS. [abstract] — [Elvira, Martino, Robert, 2018 (International Statistical Review 2022), "Rethinking the Effective Sample Size"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04129)
- With epsilon the minimum probability given to every action (for epsilon-greedy, epsilon_0 / |A|) and rewards in [0,1], IPS evaluates K policies simultaneously with confidence-interval size sqrt((C / (epsilon N)) * log(K / delta)), C a small constant; the same number of policies by A/B testing could cost error sqrt((C K / N) log(K / delta)). Randomisation "need not be uniform and for best performance should not be". [full text, partial; formula reconstructed from a two-column extraction] — [Agarwal, Bird, Cozowicz, Hoang, Langford, Lee, Li, Melamed, Oshri, Ribas, Sen, Slivkins, 2016, arXiv, "Making Contextual Decisions with Low Technical Debt"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03966)
- The offline estimator's variance has the form E[r_{pi(x)}^2 (1 / p_{pi(x)} - 1)], so "the variance is smaller when we place more probability mass to actions that are chosen by policy pi"; when the target is unknown in advance, minimising worst-case variance leads to a uniform distribution; a more conservative option is to inject randomisation around the production baseline; keep propensities above a floor p_min. [full text; the variance formula is from a garbled extraction and may omit a 1/n factor] — [Li, Chen, Kleban, Gupta, 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1891)
- Minimax MSE order (E_mu[rho^2 sigma^2] + E_mu[rho^2 Rmax^2]) / n in the agnostic case; (E_mu[rho^2 sigma^2] + Var_x E_pi[r|x]) / n with a consistent reward model. [full text] — [Wang, Agarwal, Dudik, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01205)
- IPS variance and MSE rose more than 300-fold when actions went from 10 to 5,000 at fixed n. [full text, partial] — [Saito, Joachims, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.06317)
- Policy mismatch dominated horizon in a controlled comparison (about 100x versus 10x loss of accuracy). [full text, partial] — [Voloshin et al., 2019/2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06854)
- Design of logging policies: mixing the production policy with uniform exploration is safe but "sub-optimal in maximizing information gain"; a water-filling design is proposed. [abstract] — [Zhu, Kveton, 2021, arXiv (AISTATS 2022, venue not on record), "Safe Data Collection for Offline and Online Policy Learning"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04835). Efficient and safe data collection specifically for policy evaluation and comparison. [abstract] — [Wan, Kveton, Song, 2022, arXiv (ICML 2022, venue not on record), "Safe Exploration for Efficient Policy Evaluation and Comparison"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13234). Minimum-variance augmentation logging designs the next logging policy to best complement an existing log for given target policies. [abstract] — [Tucker, Joachims, 2022, arXiv (WSDM 2023, venue not on record), "Variance-Optimal Augmentation Logging for Counterfactual Evaluation in Contextual Bandits"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01721). A well-chosen behaviour policy can give lower MSE than on-policy Monte Carlo evaluation. [abstract] — [Hanna, Thomas, Stone, Niekum, 2017, ICML, "Data-Efficient Policy Evaluation Through Behavior Policy Search"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03469)
- Gated deployment with a confidence sequence allows monitoring as data arrive and stopping when the sequence excludes the baseline, without invalidating coverage. [full text, partial] — [Karampatziakis, Mineiro, Ramdas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09540)
- Importance-sampling reliability by Pareto tail index: estimates are reliable when k-hat < 0.7; for k < 0.5 the central limit theorem holds with the usual S^(-1/2) rate; between 0.5 and 1 convergence is slower (about S^(k-1)). [full text, partial] — [Vehtari, Simpson, Gelman, Yao, Gabry, 2024, JMLR 25(72), "Pareto Smoothed Importance Sampling"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02646)
- "Make sure the effective sample sizes used to evaluate policies is large enough for our evaluations to be statistically significant"; restricting to policies close to the behaviour policy raises ESS. [full text, partial] — [Gottesman et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12298)

### Inferences
All numbers below are my arithmetic from the cited formulas, for a deterministic target policy; they are not published figures.

- **Per-decision ESS under epsilon-greedy around a rule.** Logging probability of the rule's action is 1 - epsilon + epsilon/K; of any other action epsilon/K. If the target disagrees with the rule on a fraction d of decisions, E[w^2] = (1 - d) / (1 - epsilon + epsilon/K) + d * K / epsilon and ESS / n = 1 / E[w^2].

| epsilon = 0.3 | d = 0.1 | d = 0.2 | d = 0.3 | d = 0.5 | d = 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K = 5: ESS / n | 0.35 | 0.23 | 0.17 | 0.11 | 0.060 |
| K = 8: ESS / n | 0.26 | 0.16 | 0.11 | 0.071 | 0.038 |

  Maximum weights are 16.7 (K = 5) and 26.7 (K = 8). Going from five to eight actions at the same epsilon cuts ESS by roughly a third to 40% for d of 0.2 or more.
- **Reading the reported ESS of 90 to 200.** 5,000 independent decisions at d between 0.2 and 0.5 would give ESS of about 560 to 1,140. Applying the same ratios to 950 sessions gives about 105 to 217. The reported range is therefore what one would expect if the effective unit is the session, which is consistent with the shared reward-to-go. This is a hypothesis to check against how the ESS was computed.
- **Variance of the difference (learned minus rule).** Each decision's IPS summand is zero where the two policies agree; where they disagree it is r * K / epsilon with probability epsilon/K, about -r / (1 - epsilon + epsilon/K) with the rule's probability, and zero otherwise. Per-decision variance is therefore approximately d * [(K / epsilon) * E(r^2 | candidate's action) + E(r^2 | rule's action) / (1 - epsilon + epsilon/K)] - Delta^2. With DR, r is replaced by the residual r - q-hat, shrinking the dominant term by roughly the fraction of reward variance the model does not explain.
- **Sample size from the existing data.** Interval half-width scales as 1 / sqrt(number of sessions). For a 95% interval to exclude zero with 80% power when the true effect is Delta, the standard normal-approximation requirement is half-width <= 0.7 * Delta (from (1.96 + 0.84) standard errors). If today's half-width is h0 on n0 sessions, the requirement is about n0 * (h0 / (0.7 * Delta))^2 sessions: about 2 x n0 if h0 equals the effect, about 8 x n0 if h0 is twice the effect. Caveat: using the current point estimate as Delta is optimistic when it was selected for being large; plan for a smaller effect. A direct planning formula is sessions = 7.85 * Var(session summand) / Delta^2, with the session-summand variance estimated from the current log; this automatically includes the within-session correlation.
- **Changing the logging policy, per logged decision (K = 5).**
  - Raising epsilon: for large d, ESS / n is about epsilon / (d * K); doubling epsilon to 0.6 roughly doubles ESS on disagreeing decisions, at the cost of more random actions.
  - Uniform exploration on a slice of traffic: E[w^2] = K, so ESS / n = 1/K (0.20 at K = 5, 0.125 at K = 8) for every deterministic target. This beats epsilon = 0.3 greedy once d exceeds about 0.24 (K = 5) or 0.26 (K = 8), and is target-agnostic, so the slice can be reused for later candidates.
  - Candidate-aware logging: mix the candidate's action in with probability alpha (rule, candidate and a small uniform floor). Weights for the candidate are then at most about 1/alpha and for the rule at most about 1/(1 - alpha); at alpha = 0.5 both ESS ratios are near 0.5. This is defensive mixture sampling and the direction Li et al. and Tucker and Joachims point to. It does mean the candidate acts on some traffic before the gate is passed, so it only fits if a bounded pilot is acceptable.
  - Session-level randomised pilot (each session follows the rule or the candidate throughout): no importance weights, the session value is observed directly, sequential effects are included, and a two-arm comparison needs about 2 * (1.96 + 0.84)^2 * sigma_session^2 / Delta^2 sessions per arm (textbook two-sample formula; no opened source). This is the only design here that measures the full-session value without products of weights or model assumptions.
- **Sequential evaluation.** For trajectory-level weights the per-step second moments multiply (section 2), so no realistic amount of epsilon = 0.3 logging supports unbiased full-session OPE of a policy that often departs from the rule; planning for that estimand should assume a randomised pilot or session-level candidate-aware logging.
- **Stopping rule.** If data arrive over time, a confidence sequence allows checking after every batch and stopping as soon as zero is excluded; fixed-sample intervals re-checked repeatedly do not keep their coverage.

### Gaps
- I found no published rule of thumb of the form "ESS must exceed X" for OPE; Owen states the threshold is application-specific, and the PSIS k-hat < 0.7 rule comes from Bayesian computation, not OPE.
- No opened source gives a closed-form ESS for epsilon-greedy logging as a function of epsilon, K and disagreement; the formula above is a direct calculation from the definition N / E[w^2], not a citation.
- A 2026 ICML paper surfaced in search results, arguing that the normalised-weight ESS proxy alone is incomplete because it ignores reward variance ([listing](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/83653)); **not opened**, title and authors unverified.
- The Decision Service formula was reconstructed from a column-scrambled text extraction; the epsilon in the denominator is visible in the raw extraction but the layout should be checked against the PDF before quoting.
- Zhu and Kveton, Wan et al., Tucker and Joachims, and Hanna et al. were read at abstract level; their quantitative gains were not extracted.

## 6. Pitfalls reported in practice: propensity checks, weight diagnostics, support checks, selection on the evaluation set

### Takeaway
The failures reported most often are wrong or mis-logged propensities, a handful of observations carrying the estimate, and choosing the policy or the estimator on the same data used to evaluate it. Each has a cheap diagnostic: propensity audit tests, weight and effective-sample-size inspection (at session level), and a held-out evaluation split fixed in advance.

### Cited Findings

**Propensity and logging correctness**
- "Any errors in the calculation and/or logging of the scores can lead to a bias"; verification "turns out to be one of the most critical, and sometimes challenging, steps". Two checks: log the randomisation seed and replay the draw; or run statistical tests. The arithmetic mean test compares how often an action appears with its expected count given logged propensities. The harmonic mean test uses the identity E[1(a = a*) / p_{a*} + 1(a != a*) / (1 - p_{a*})] = 2 for any context and action a*. The tests detect "some but not all data issues". [full text] — [Li, Chen, Kleban, Gupta, 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1891)
- Common production failures: logging "not the action chosen by the ML algorithm, but the outcome at the end of the pipeline" (e.g. overrides), context features that change between decision time and learning time, and rewards arriving after variable delays that bias toward fast feedback; remedy is to log (x, a, r, p) at decision time. [full text, partial] — [Agarwal et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03966)
- The condition E[w] = 1 is used as a control variate (additively in DR-style corrections, multiplicatively in SNIPS), so a sample mean weight far from 1 signals a problem. [full text, partial] — [Karampatziakis, Mineiro, Ramdas, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09540)
- When propensities must be estimated from data rather than logged, consistency rests on that model. [abstract] — [Strehl, Langford, Li, Kakade, 2010, arXiv (NeurIPS 2010, venue not on record), "Learning from Logged Implicit Exploration Data"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.0120)
- If the logging policy adapts over time, standard DR can be biased or have excessive variance; adaptive weighting gives valid t-statistics. [abstract] — [Zhan, Hadad, Hirshberg, Athey, 2021, arXiv (KDD 2021, venue not on record), "Off-Policy Evaluation via Adaptive Weighting with Data from Contextual Bandits"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02029)

**Weight diagnostics**
- "Always examine the distribution of the importance weights"; in their example the surviving trajectories were also unrepresentative (average stay 3.7 intervals against 13 in the cohort), so a small effective sample can also be a biased subsample. [full text, partial] — [Gottesman et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12298)
- The relative sizes of the inner (exploration-limited) and outer (sample-size-limited) intervals under clipping tell whether to collect more of the same data or change the exploration. [full text] — [Bottou et al., 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.2355)
- ESS, weight skewness and an f-specific ESS are complementary diagnostics; none proves success. [full text, partial] — [Owen, Chapter 9](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/Ch-var-is.pdf). Pareto k-hat as a tail diagnostic. [full text, partial] — [Vehtari et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02646)
- Weighted (self-normalised) estimators are biased toward the behaviour policy's value when few samples carry weight: with one trajectory WIS is an unbiased estimate of the behaviour policy's value. [full text, partial] — [Hanna, Stone, Niekum, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06126); [Thomas, Brunskill, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00923); the same point explains optimistic WIS-based validation in small data — [Nie et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08642)

**Support / overlap**
- IPS is biased when the logging policy lacks support for actions the target takes, and learning can "fail catastrophically". [abstract] — [Sachdeva, Su, Joachims, 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09438)
- HCOPE notes the lower bound remains valid even where the evaluation policy has support the behaviour policy lacks (an appendix result), because missing mass only lowers the estimate. [full text, partial] — [Thomas, Theocharous, Ghavamzadeh, 2015, AAAI](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~pthomas/papers/Thomas2015.pdf)

**Selection on the evaluation set**
- For IS and DR to stay unbiased the target policy "should not depend on the samples used" in the estimate; the reward model must be independent of them as well. [full text] — [Jiang, Li, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03722)
- Searching for a policy and testing it on the same data "has a tendency to overfit"; hence the candidate/safety split. [full text] — [Thomas, Theocharous, Ghavamzadeh, 2015, ICML](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~pthomas/papers/Thomas2015b.pdf)
- OPE-driven hyperparameter search selects overestimated configurations. [abstract] — [Saito, Nomura, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15084). With small data, results depend strongly on the particular train/validation split. [full text, partial] — [Nie et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08642)
- OPE is commonly used to shortlist policies for online tests; estimators should be judged by the risk-return of the shortlist, not accuracy alone. [abstract] — [Kiyohara, Kishimoto, Kawakami, Kobayashi, Nakata, Saito, 2024, ICLR, "Towards Assessing and Benchmarking Risk-Return Tradeoff of Off-Policy Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18207)

**Modelling assumptions**
- Guarantees assume the environment is a POMDP that does not change; if the domain is non-stationary the guarantees "are only approximate". [full text] — [Thomas, Theocharous, Ghavamzadeh, 2015, ICML](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~pthomas/papers/Thomas2015b.pdf)
- State representation and reward design can drive apparent policy gains in observational RL evaluation. [abstract and partial text] — [Gottesman et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12298)
- The direct method's misspecification bias "cannot be easily quantified from data". [docs] — [Open Bandit Pipeline documentation](https://zr-obp.readthedocs.io/en/latest/estimators.html)

### Inferences
- A pre-evaluation checklist that follows from the above: (1) recompute each logged propensity from the rule's action and epsilon and confirm it matches the log; run the arithmetic and harmonic mean tests per action; check that the logged action is the one actually executed; (2) check the mean importance weight is close to 1 for every candidate, overall and per session-length stratum; (3) compute ESS at session level, the share of total weight held by the top 1% and top 5 sessions, and a tail index; (4) confirm every action the candidate takes has non-zero logged probability in that context (true by construction under epsilon-greedy, but worth asserting in code); (5) freeze candidate policy, reward model, estimator and its hyperparameters on a training split, and compute the gate interval once on the held-out sessions, splitting by session so no session straddles both.
- Because decisions where the candidate agrees with the rule contribute nothing to the difference, the informative sample is the set of sessions with at least one disagreement; reporting that count alongside ESS makes thin evidence visible.
- With several candidate policies or several estimators tried, the chance that at least one interval excludes zero by luck rises; either pre-register one candidate and one estimator or use a simultaneous bound (the log(K/delta) factor in the Decision Service bound shows the cost is mild).
- If the rule or epsilon changed during logging, the data are from several logging policies; per-decision logged propensities keep IPS valid, but i.i.d.-based intervals should be replaced by martingale-valid ones.

### Gaps
- No opened source reports the frequency of propensity-logging bugs; the evidence is practitioner testimony (Li et al., Agarwal et al., who note such failures "are typically not published").
- I did not find a published test for the case here, where the propensity is a deterministic function of the rule's action and epsilon; the cited tests are general.
- Mandel et al. (2014, AAMAS, offline evaluation across representations) and the SIGIR 2016 / RecSys 2021 counterfactual-evaluation tutorials were **not opened**.
- Currency: searches were run on 15 November 2026. The newest sources opened are from 2024 (Sakhi et al.; Saito and Nomura; Cief et al.; Felicioni et al.) and the JMLR 2024 version of PSIS. Software documentation was read for Vowpal Wabbit 9.6.0, Open Bandit Pipeline "latest" and SCOPE-RL "latest"; any of these may lag the current releases. Work published in 2025 to 2026 on betting-based or clustered OPE inference, if any, is not covered.
